Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

In-depth, technically verified answers regarding the AegisMotion pose estimation pipeline, 3D kinematics, and cloud operations.

Click the "Camera ON" button on the top toolbar or the "Turn Camera ON" button inside the AI Vision Lab box. Your browser will request access to your webcam (via the native HTML5 navigator.mediaDevices.getUserMedia API). Once granted, live video frames are delivered directly to Google MediaPipe Pose, executing real-time landmark extraction locally in your browser at full speed with complete data privacy.
Click the "Upload Video" button or directly drag-and-drop any pre-recorded video file into the dashed dropzone area. AegisMotion immediately creates a secure local Blob Object URL, displays full playback controls (Play, Pause, Scrub Bar, Restart, and Loop Toggle), and feeds every decoded video frame into the pose estimation network for continuous telemetry.
Use the "Camera ON" and "Camera OFF" toggle buttons at the top of the AI Vision card. Clicking "Camera OFF" safely releases all active hardware tracks via MediaStreamTrack.stop(), cancels the requestAnimationFrame loop, turns off camera indicators, and returns the viewport to standby mode without reloading the page.
AegisMotion supports all major browser-native video codecs and containers including MP4 (H.264/AAC), WebM (VP8/VP9), AVI, and MOV. For best biomechanical accuracy, ensure the human subject is fully visible from head to feet in reasonable lighting.
AegisMotion executes a multi-stage biomechanical analysis pipeline:
  1. Keypoint Extraction: MediaPipe tracks 33 3D anatomical joints across shoulders, hips, knees, and ankles.
  2. Kinematic Angle Calculation: The Torso Angle from vertical (θ = |atan2(Δx, Δy)|) and Bounding Box Aspect Ratio (Width/Height) are computed continuously.
  3. Threshold Activation: When the torso exceeds 55°, aspect ratio exceeds 0.85, and downward vertical velocity surges, Fall Probability rises past 50%.
  4. 5-Second Non-Recovery Verification: An automated Finite State Machine (FSM) enters a 5.0-second countdown. If the subject remains down and does not self-recover into a seated or standing posture, the fall is classified as confirmed, triggering siren synthesis, Supabase persistence, and Telegram dispatch.
Fall severity is classified based on impact dynamics and post-fall mobility:
  • MILD (Score 50%–65%): Slump or controlled descent with partial preservation of limb support; low velocity impact.
  • MODERATE (Score 65%–82%): Rapid transition to floor with significant kinetic impact; subject unable to recover within grace window.
  • SEVERE (Score >82%): High-velocity sudden collapse, horizontal torso angle (>75°), zero post-impact movement, and full loss of postural control requiring urgent caregiver response.
Fall probability (0% to 100%) is a mathematically weighted ensemble metric reflecting the degree to which current body kinematics align with verified ground fall trajectories. Below 20% indicates normal upright gait; 20%–50% indicates sitting, bending, or resting; and >50% activates the 5-second non-recovery alert verification pipeline.
False detections can occur during intentional floor activities (yoga, pushups, picking up objects under low furniture), extreme camera angles (such as pure top-down ceiling views that distort vertical torso projection), or when loose clothing or blankets obstruct landmark tracking. The 5-second verification grace period and the on-screen "I'm OK / Dismiss" controls prevent false alarms from triggering false emergency dispatches.
In browser client mode, Google MediaPipe Pose prioritizes tracking the primary subject closest to the focal center. In edge server mode with our Python backend (web_server.py), YOLOv8 object detection paired with ByteTrack enables multi-person tracking, tracking individuals independently as Person #1, Person #2, etc., across consecutive frames.
The interactive Three.js 3D Viewport renders a real-time digital twin mannequin of the monitored subject in true metric spatial coordinates. It visualizes anatomical joints, interconnected bone vectors, volumetric capsule limbs, an orientation radar grid, and Center of Mass plumb projections. Users can interactively rotate, zoom, and pitch the camera using mouse drag or keyboard presets (F = Front, S = Side, T = Top, R = Isometric).
The Centre of Mass (CoM) is computed using standard Dempster anthropometric segment mass proportions (torso, pelvis, head, upper and lower limbs). In the 3D viewport, CoM is rendered as an illuminated yellow sphere. A vertical plumb line projects downwards to the ground plane, indicating the subject's instantaneous dynamic balance vector.
Balance stability evaluates whether the projected CoM ground point lies inside the Base of Support (BoS) footprint formed by the subject's feet. If the projection remains inside the footprint polygon, the balance indicator reads STABLE (Green). If the CoM ground projection drifts outside the footprint boundary, it switches to UNSTABLE (Amber/Red), calculating the exact stability margin (cm) and sway velocity (m/s).
  • Torso Angle: The acute inclination angle between the mid-shoulder to mid-hip spine line and true gravitational vertical (0° = perfectly upright, 90° = horizontal).
  • Hip Flexion: The 3D interior angle between the torso vector (shoulders to hips) and the thigh vector (hips to knees). Upright standing is ~175°–180°; sitting is ~90°.
  • Knee Angle: The 3D angle between the thigh vector and the shank vector (knees to ankles). Straight standing is ~175°; seated flexion is ~90°.
When a fall incident is confirmed, AegisMotion dispatches a high-priority alert payload via the Telegram Bot API (using @AegisMotionAlertBot). The broadcast includes the resident identifier, classification severity, detection confidence, timestamp, GPS telemetry, and an immediate visual photographic snapshot taken at the moment of fall confirmation.
Snapshots captured during verified fall events are stored in Supabase cloud storage and referenced directly in the fall_events table under snapshot_url. The Cloud Incident History Ledger displays secure visual thumbnails that can be clicked to open in an expanded modal lightbox for inspection.
Click the "Export CSV Logs" button located in the Incident Ledger or Reports screen. The browser compiles all logged records into an RFC-4180 compliant CSV spreadsheet (including timestamps, severity, fall score, duration down, resolution state, and notes) and downloads it directly to your machine.
Supabase shows disconnected if the client machine loses internet connectivity or if strict enterprise firewall/proxy settings block outbound secure WebSockets (WSS) to *.supabase.co. The client automatically attempts reconnections every 3 seconds while keeping local detection and audio siren alarms active.

Technical Troubleshooting Guide

Practical, diagnostic resolutions for camera input, model inference, 3D graphics rendering, and cloud telemetry.

1. Camera Not Working
HARDWARE
The webcam does not start when clicking "Camera ON", or shows a blank black stream.
Possible Cause: Browser camera permission was blocked, or another background app (Zoom, Teams) is monopolizing the webcam.
Recommended Solution: Click the lock/camera icon in your browser address bar and select "Allow". Close other applications using your webcam, then click "Camera ON" again.
2. Video Upload Failing
MEDIA
Selected video does not load into the viewport, or player freezes on 0:00.
Possible Cause: Unsupported video codec (e.g. raw HEVC/H.265 not supported in standard browsers) or corrupted file container.
Recommended Solution: Convert the video to standard MP4 with H.264 video and AAC audio encoding. Verify file plays in your local media player before re-uploading.
3. Low FPS or Slow Processing
PERFORMANCE
Throughput counter drops below 15 FPS, causing stuttering or latency in fall detection.
Possible Cause: Hardware acceleration disabled in browser settings, or high-resolution video overload.
Recommended Solution: Navigate to browser Settings > System and enable "Use graphics acceleration when available". In Brave, set Shields Fingerprinting to Standard.
4. Incorrect Fall Detection
AI VISION
System triggers a fall alert while the resident is sitting on a low couch or bending over.
Possible Cause: Camera angle positioned too low or too steep, causing optical foreshortening of torso vectors.
Recommended Solution: Mount camera at chest height (1.2m–1.5m) angled slightly downward. Allow the 5-second non-recovery window or press "I'm OK" to clear.
5. Multiple People Not Being Detected
PIPELINE
Only one skeleton is drawn even when two or more individuals enter the room.
Possible Cause: Browser-native MediaPipe Pose operates in single-subject focus mode by default for zero-latency execution.
Recommended Solution: For institutional multi-resident tracking, run the backend server pipeline (python main.py --source 0), which enables YOLOv8 multi-person tracking with ByteTrack IDs.
6. 3D Skeleton Not Appearing
GRAPHICS
The 3D Metric Skeleton Viewport card appears blank or shows a black canvas without grid lines.
Possible Cause: WebGL context lost or browser fingerprinting protection blocking canvas 3D context initialization.
Recommended Solution: Click the Isometric (R) button to re-render the camera viewport. AegisMotion includes an automated 2D Perspective Canvas fallback that activates automatically if WebGL fails.
7. Incorrect Biomechanical Measurements
METRICS
Torso angle or knee flexion values fluctuate erratically while standing still.
Possible Cause: Poor lighting causing landmark jitter, or lower limbs occluded behind furniture or desks.
Recommended Solution: Ensure room illumination is even and unobstructed. Ensure the subject's feet, knees, and hips are clearly visible within the camera's field of view.
8. Telegram Notifications Not Working
NOTIFICATIONS
Confirmed fall occurs but no Telegram notification arrives on caregiver mobile devices.
Possible Cause: Caregiver has not initiated a conversation with the Telegram bot or incorrect chat ID configured.
Recommended Solution: Open Telegram, search for @AegisMotionAlertBot, and tap /start. Verify your Chat ID is authorized in alert_config.json.
9. Supabase Connection Failure
CLOUD DATABASE
Top status indicator displays "Supabase Disconnected" or incidents fail to persist.
Possible Cause: Network outage, enterprise proxy blocking WebSocket traffic, or invalid Anon Key credentials.
Recommended Solution: Test internet connectivity. Ensure outbound traffic to *.supabase.co on port 443 is unrestricted. Verify SUPABASE_URL in configuration.
10. Incident History Not Updating
LEDGER
New fall events do not appear in the Incident Ledger table in real-time.
Possible Cause: Supabase Realtime channel subscription paused, or cached browser tab state.
Recommended Solution: Click the "Refresh" button located in the Incident Ledger card header. This triggers an immediate fresh SQL query against the fall_events table.

Getting Started

Introduction to AegisMotion AI: An advanced, cloud-connected fall detection and postural analytics platform combining deep learning computer vision, 3D kinematic modeling, and instantaneous emergency notification channels.

Incident Management & Auditing

Every confirmed fall triggers an immutable audit log synchronized to the Supabase cloud ledger.

  • Severity Tiers: Color-coded MILD (Amber), MODERATE (Orange), and SEVERE (Red) classifications.
  • Snapshot Ledger: Photographic evidence captures stored securely with lightbox thumbnail viewing.
  • Resolving Incidents: Caregivers can acknowledge and resolve active alerts with timestamped resolution notes.
  • CSV Export: One-click download of clinical records for medical reviews and compliance.

Understanding Dashboard Telemetry Metrics (14 Clinical Indicators)

1. Current Posture: Real-time classified state (Standing, Sitting, Bending, Lying Down, FALLEN).
2. Fall Probability: 0%–100% ensemble kinematic risk score combining angle, velocity, and aspect ratio.
3. Torso Angle: Spinal inclination angle relative to gravitational vertical (0° = vertical, 90° = horizontal).
4. Hip Flexion: 3D angle between torso and thigh vectors (175° = standing, ~90° = seated).
5. Knee Angle: 3D angle between thigh and shank vectors (175° = extended, ~90° = flexed).
6. Standing Time: Accumulated cumulative duration in upright posture during active session.
7. Sitting Time: Accumulated cumulative time spent seated in resting or desk posture.
8. Walking / Active Movement: Time spent undergoing dynamic locomotion and postural transitions.
9. Upright Standing: Time spent maintaining upright posture with base-of-support stability.
10. Seated / Resting: Resting sedentary periods tracked for clinical mobility evaluation.
11. Lying Down: Time spent recumbent in horizontal orientation.
12. Active vs Sedentary Ratio: Proportional balance bar comparing active vs inactive hours.
13. Continuous Inactivity Duration: Running stopwatch alerting if sedentary interval exceeds 45m.
14. Sit ↔ Stand Transitions & Mobility Score: Functional movement count with dynamic 0–100 mobility score.

Understanding 3D Biomechanical Viewport (12 Interactive Layers)

Isometric View (R): 45° 3D perspective camera.
Front View (F): Coronal plane gait perspective.
Side View (S): Sagittal spinal angle perspective.
Top View (T): Transverse Base-of-Support view.
3D Mesh Toggle: Volumetric anatomical mannequin.
Bones Toggle: High-contrast kinematic linkages.
Centre of Mass (CoM): Yellow Dempster CoM sphere.
Motion Trail Toggle: Dynamic kinetic ribbon path.
Radar Grid Toggle: Polar concentric ground coordinates.
Balance Stability: STABLE (Green) vs UNSTABLE (Red).
Balance Margin: Linear distance (cm) to BoS boundary.
Velocity: Instantaneous CoM linear velocity (m/s).
